# Phase 5: User & Community Intelligence

## Primary Persona: "Adventure Parent Alex"

### Demographics
| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Age | 30-45 |
| Children | 1-3 kids, ages 5-13 |
| Background | Played D&D as teen/adult OR always wanted to try |
| Tech Comfort | High - uses apps daily |
| Income | Middle class, willing to pay for quality family activities |

### Core Profile

**One-liner:** Geeky parent who wants to share the magic of D&D with their kids but lacks time, skills, and kid-appropriate tools.

**Core Job:** Create magical bonding moments with my kids through imaginative play.

**Biggest Pain:** "I want to DM for my kids but I don't have time to prep, don't know how to simplify rules, and worry I'll bore them or mess it up."

**Dream Outcome:** Weekly family adventures where my kids beg to play again, we laugh together, and they develop creativity and problem-solving skills.

**Price Tolerance:** $10-15/month for something that actually works.

**Defining Quote:**
> "The day that my kids would ask me to play one of my monster games had finally come. I have held in my deepest and geekiest heart-of-hearts that my kids would show an interest in tabletop role-playing."

---

## Secondary Personas

### New-to-RPG Parent
- **Description:** Never played D&D but kids are obsessed after seeing Stranger Things
- **Core Need:** "Guide me through EVERYTHING - I don't know what a d20 is"
- **Key Quote:** "My kid overhears your D&D game, sees it played in Stranger Things, or comes home all excited... and they ask you if they can play D&D too! Now what?"

### Busy Professional Parent
- **Description:** Wants quality family time but has zero prep time
- **Core Need:** Ready-to-play adventures I can run in 30-60 minutes
- **Key Quote:** "I barely have time to prep for my regular campaign, having pre-written adventures available is fantastic"

### Screen-Concerned Parent
- **Description:** Worried about kids' screen addiction, seeking alternatives
- **Core Need:** Technology that brings us together, not apart
- **Key Quote:** "Do your kids live on their tablets? We try to limit ours but it can be hard"

---

## Jobs to Be Done

### Functional Jobs
- Help me run a D&D-style adventure for kids aged 7-13
- Generate age-appropriate story content on-demand
- Simplify complex RPG rules so kids can understand
- Track characters, inventory, and story progress
- Read the story aloud so I can focus on facilitating
- Show images so kids can visualize the adventure
- Let us use physical dice we already own

### Emotional Jobs
- Feel like a great parent creating magical moments
- Relive my own childhood love of fantasy/gaming
- Feel confident I'm doing it "right" without being an expert
- Pride in seeing my kids use their imagination
- Relief that screen time is actually beneficial

### Social Jobs
- Be the "cool parent" who plays D&D with kids
- Create shared family stories and inside jokes
- Give kids something to brag about to friends
- Connect with other geeky parents

## Behavioral Patterns

### Usage Context
| Dimension | Pattern |
|-----------|---------|
| When | Weekends, rainy days, family game nights |
| Where | Living room, kitchen table, sometimes car trips |
| Duration | 30-60 minutes ideal for young kids, up to 90 for older |
| Frequency | 1-2x per week when engaged |

### Decision Factors
1. Kid-safe content (NO inappropriate content)
2. Easy to start (low learning curve)
3. Price justified by engagement
4. Works for MY specific kids' ages
5. Reviews from other parents

### Triggers to Buy
- Kids see D&D in media (Stranger Things, etc.)
- Friend's kids are playing
- Looking for screen-time alternatives
- Rainy weekend with nothing planned
- Holiday gift for family activity

### Barriers to Adoption
- Is it actually kid-safe?
- Will my kids actually engage?
- Is it worth the subscription?
- Do I need to know D&D already?

### Price Sensitivity
| Anchor | Price |
|--------|-------|
| Netflix for kids | ~$7/mo |
| One board game | ~$30 |
| D&D facilitator | $50-150/session |
| **Acceptable range** | **$5-15/month** |
| **Willingness** | HIGH if it delivers magical family moments |

## Key Insights

| Insight | Implication |
|---------|-------------|
| Parents are buyers, kids are users - but parents only pay if kids engage AND parents feel confident | Need parent-mode (controls, guidance) AND kid-mode (fun, visual) |
| Physical props (dice, candy, minis) dramatically increase engagement | Physical dice integration is MAJOR differentiator |
| Kids want to replay adventures AND continue stories | Save progress, allow replays, build ongoing campaigns |
| Attention spans are SHORT (30-60 min max for young kids) | Design for short sessions with natural pause points |
| Parents fear "doing it wrong" more than anything | AI should be encouraging, guide parents, never shame |
| Pets and companions are universally loved by kid players | Include pet companions as core feature |

## Language Guidelines

### Marketing - DO Say
- "Family adventure time"
- "Your kids will beg to play again"
- "No D&D experience needed"
- "Screen time that brings you together"
- "Magical moments, zero prep"
- "AI-powered DM assistant"
- "Use your own dice"

### Marketing - DON'T Say
- "AI Dungeon Master" (sounds like it replaces parent)
- "Educational" (turns kids off)
- "Simplified" (sounds dumbed down)
- "Safe" (sounds boring)
- "Parental controls" (sounds restrictive)

### Product UI - DO Say
- "Roll your dice and tell me what you got!"
- "What does [character name] want to do?"
- "You did it! The dragon retreats!"
- "Time for a snack break?"

### Product UI - DON'T Say
- "Invalid input"
- "You failed"
- "Game over"
- "Error"

## User Segments Priority

### Priority 1: Geeky Parents with Kids 7-10
- **Why First:** Most motivated, understand value, will evangelize
- **Size:** Largest segment

### Priority 2: New-to-RPG Parents with Curious Kids
- **Why Second:** Growing fast (Stranger Things effect), need more onboarding

### Priority 3: Homeschool Families
- **Why Later:** Niche but passionate, great for word-of-mouth

### Anti-Persona: Hardcore D&D Players
- **Why Not:** Will find it too simple, will complain, wrong audience
